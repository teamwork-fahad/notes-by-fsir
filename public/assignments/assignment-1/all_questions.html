📌 Question 1: Find Mean, Median and Mode
Problem Statement: Find the mean, median and mode from the following data:
Class0–1010–2020–3030–4040–5050–60
Frequency46201073
💡 Easy Guide / आसान तरीका:
1. Mean ($\bar{x}$) ke liye mid-value $x_i$ nikalo, $f_i x_i$ ka sum karo aur $N$ se divide karo.
2. Median ke liye $\frac{N}{2} = 25$ dhoondo aur median class formula use karo.
3. Mode ke liye sabse badi frequency ($20$) wali modal class chuno aur formula lagao.
1 Step 1: Construct Calculation Table

Total $N = 50$ — $\sum f_i x_i = 1440$ —
2 Step 2: Calculate Arithmetic Mean ($\bar{x}$)
$$\bar{x} = \frac{\sum f_i x_i}{N}$$ $$\bar{x} = \frac{1440}{50} = \mathbf{28.8}$$
3 Step 3: Calculate Median

1. Position of Median: $\frac{N}{2} = \frac{50}{2} = 25$.

2. Cumulative frequency just greater than or equal to $25$ is 30 $\implies$ Median Class = 20–30.

  • Lower limit of median class ($L$) = $20$
  • $cf$ of preceding class ($cf$) = $10$
  • Frequency of median class ($f$) = $20$
  • Class length ($h$) = $10$
$$\text{Median} = L + \left( \frac{\frac{N}{2} - cf}{f} \right) \times h$$ $$\text{Median} = 20 + \left( \frac{25 - 10}{20} \right) \times 10 = 20 + \frac{15}{2} = 20 + 7.5 = \mathbf{27.5}$$
4 Step 4: Calculate Mode

Maximum frequency is $20$ $\implies$ Modal Class = 20–30.

  • $L = 20$
  • Frequency of modal class ($f_1$) = $20$
  • Frequency of previous class ($f_0$) = $6$
  • Frequency of next class ($f_2$) = $10$
  • Class width ($h$) = $10$
$$\text{Mode} = L + \left( \frac{f_1 - f_0}{2f_1 - f_0 - f_2} \right) \times h$$ $$\text{Mode} = 20 + \left( \frac{20 - 6}{2(20) - 6 - 10} \right) \times 10 = 20 + \left( \frac{14}{40 - 16} \right) \times 10$$ $$\text{Mode} = 20 + \frac{140}{24} = 20 + 5.833 = \mathbf{25.833} \quad \left( \text{or } 25\frac{5}{6} \right)$$

✅ Final Answer:

  • Mean ($\bar{x}$) = 28.8
  • Median = 27.5
  • Mode = 25.833 (or $25\frac{5}{6}$)

📌 Question 4: Calculate Coefficient of Variation (C.V.)
Problem Statement: Calculate the co-efficient of variation (C.V.) from the following data:
Age (in years)0–910–1920–2930–3940–4950–59
No. of persons ($f$)13386776224
💡 Easy Guide / आसान तरीका:
- Inclusive class intervals (0-9, 10-19) ke mid-values hote hain: $\frac{0+9}{2} = 4.5, 14.5, 24.5 \dots$
- Step-deviation method se calculation easy ho jaati hai: $A = 24.5, h = 10, u_i = \frac{x_i - 24.5}{10}$.
- Formula: $C.V. = \frac{\sigma}{\bar{x}} \times 100$.
1 Step 1: Step-Deviation Calculation Table

Let Assumed Mean $A = 24.5$, width $h = 10$. $u_i = \frac{x_i - 24.5}{10}$.

Age (Class) $f_i$ Mid-point ($x_i$) $u_i$ $f_i u_i$ $u_i^2$ $f_i u_i^2$

Total $N = 220$ — — $\sum f_i u_i = 68$ — $\sum f_i u_i^2 = 290$
2 Step 2: Calculate Mean ($\bar{x}$)
$$\bar{x} = A + \left( \frac{\sum f_i u_i}{N} \right) \times h$$ $$\bar{x} = 24.5 + \left( \frac{68}{220} \right) \times 10 = 24.5 + 3.0909 = \mathbf{27.5909 \text{ years}}$$
3 Step 3: Calculate Standard Deviation ($\sigma$)
$$\sigma = h \times \sqrt{ \frac{\sum f_i u_i^2}{N} - \left( \frac{\sum f_i u_i}{N} \right)^2 }$$ $$\frac{\sum f_i u_i^2}{N} = \frac{290}{220} \approx 1.318182$$ $$\left( \frac{\sum f_i u_i}{N} \right)^2 = \left( \frac{68}{220} \right)^2 = (0.309091)^2 \approx 0.095537$$ $$\text{Variance in } u = 1.318182 - 0.095537 = 1.222645$$ $$\sigma = 10 \times \sqrt{1.222645} = 10 \times 1.105733 = \mathbf{11.0573 \text{ years}}$$
4 Step 4: Calculate Coefficient of Variation (C.V.)
$$C.V. = \frac{\sigma}{\bar{x}} \times 100$$ $$C.V. = \frac{11.0573}{27.5909} \times 100 \approx \mathbf{40.08\%}$$

✅ Final Answer:

  • Mean ($\bar{x}$) = 27.59 years
  • Standard Deviation ($\sigma$) = 11.06 years

📌 Question 13: Statistical Theory & Definitions
Problem Statement:
  • Define Range.
  • Define Mean Deviation.
  • Define Standard Deviation.
  • State Measures of Dispersion.
  • State Merits and Demerits of Mean, Median, and Mode.

1. Definition of Range

Definition: Range is the simplest and most elementary measure of dispersion. It is defined as the absolute difference between the largest (maximum) observation and the smallest (minimum) observation present in a given dataset.

$$\text{Range} = L - S$$ $$\text{Coefficient of Range} = \frac{L - S}{L + S}$$ where $L = \text{Largest value}$, $S = \text{Smallest value}$.

2. Definition of Mean Deviation

Definition: Mean Deviation (or Average Deviation) is the arithmetic mean of the absolute values of the numerical deviations of observations taken from an average (usually the arithmetic mean or median). Algebraic signs (+ or -) are deliberately ignored.

$$\text{For Individual Series: } MD = \frac{\sum |x_i - A|}{n}$$ $$\text{For Grouped Series: } MD = \frac{\sum f_i |x_i - A|}{N}$$ $$\text{Coefficient of } MD = \frac{MD}{A}$$ where $A$ is the Mean ($\bar{x}$) or Median ($M$).

3. Definition of Standard Deviation

Definition: Standard Deviation was introduced by Karl Pearson in 1893. It is defined as the positive square root of the arithmetic mean of the squared deviations of observations taken from their arithmetic mean. It is universally denoted by the Greek letter $\sigma$ (sigma).

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{N}} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum f_i x_i^2}{N} - \left(\frac{\sum f_i x_i}{N}\right)^2}$$ $$\text{Variance} = \sigma^2$$

4. State Measures of Dispersion

Dispersion represents the extent to which values in a distribution scatter around a central tendency. They are categorized into:

A. Absolute Measures (Have units)
  1. Range ($L - S$)
  2. Quartile Deviation ($QD = \frac{Q_3 - Q_1}{2}$)
  3. Mean Deviation
  4. Standard Deviation & Variance
B. Relative Measures (Unit-free)
  1. Coefficient of Range = $\frac{L - S}{L + S}$
  2. Coefficient of QD = $\frac{Q_3 - Q_1}{Q_3 + Q_1}$
  3. Coefficient of MD = $\frac{MD}{\text{Mean/Median}}$
  4. Coefficient of Variation (C.V.) = $\frac{\sigma}{\bar{x}} \times 100$

5. Merits and Demerits of Mean, Median, and Mode

A. Arithmetic Mean ($\bar{x}$)
✅ Merits
  • Rigidly defined by a mathematical formula.
  • Based on all observations of the dataset.
  • Capable of further algebraic treatment (e.g., combined mean).
  • Least affected by sampling fluctuations (most stable).
❌ Demerits
  • Highly sensitive to extreme values (outliers).
  • Cannot be calculated for open-ended classes without assumptions.
  • Cannot be determined graphically.
  • Cannot be measured for qualitative data (honesty, beauty).
B. Median ($M$)
✅ Merits
  • Easy to understand and compute.
  • Completely unaffected by extreme values / outliers.
  • Can be calculated for open-ended frequency distributions.
  • Can be determined graphically using Ogive curves.
  • Ideal measure for qualitative data.
❌ Demerits
  • Not based on all items (depends only on middle position).
  • Not amenable to algebraic treatment (cannot combine medians).
  • Requires sorting/ranking of data (laborious for large data).
  • Less stable under sampling fluctuations than mean.
C. Mode ($Z$)
✅ Merits
  • Easiest to understand; can often be determined by inspection.
  • Unaffected by extreme values.
  • Can be computed for open-ended classes.
  • Can be determined graphically using a Histogram.
  • Very practical for trade and industry (popular sizes).
❌ Demerits
  • Ill-defined in bimodal, multimodal, or uniform distributions.
  • Not based on all observations in the dataset.
  • Not capable of further algebraic manipulation.
  • Most unstable average under sampling fluctuations.
