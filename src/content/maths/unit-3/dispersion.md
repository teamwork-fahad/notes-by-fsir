---
title: '3.5 Measures of Dispersion'
description: 'Methods used to describe how far observations spread around a central value.'
---

Dispersion measures how far observations spread around a central value.

## 3.5.1 Range

$Range=Maximum-Minimum$. It is simple but strongly affected by extreme values.

## 3.5.2 Quartile Deviation

$QD=\dfrac{Q_3-Q_1}{2}$. It describes the spread of the middle half of the data.

## 3.5.3 Mean Deviation

Mean deviation is the average of absolute deviations from a chosen central value: $MD=\dfrac{\sum|x-a|}{n}$.

## 3.5.4 Standard Deviation

$\sigma=\sqrt{\dfrac{\sum(x-\bar{x})^2}{n}}$. Variance is $\sigma^2$.

## 3.5.5 Skewness and Kurtosis

Skewness measures asymmetry. Kurtosis measures the concentration and tail shape relative to a normal distribution; distributions may be mesokurtic, leptokurtic, or platykurtic.
